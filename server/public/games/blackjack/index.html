Start solo or share a table

  1. Choose Play Blackjack now. Select one deck, or enable two decks before creating the room.
  2. Choose your name. You may start alone, or share the five-character code with up to three friends.
  3. The host starts the table. Players act in turn against the dealer; one player standing does not end everyone else's hand.

The starting 1,000 chips and every wager are virtual, with no cash value or redemption. The host may enable public discovery. Saved sessions reconnect before a new game link.

Get closer to 21 without going over

Number cards score their number; tens and face cards score ten. An ace counts as eleven when possible and one when needed to avoid busting. Each eligible player automatically bets 50 chips and receives two cards.

  • Hit: draw another card. Going over 21 busts; reaching 21 ends your action automatically.
  • Stand: keep your total and let the next player act.
  • Double: with exactly two initial cards and enough chips, add another 50, take exactly one card, and stand. Your total wager becomes 100.

The dealer's hole card stays hidden while players act. The dealer draws below 17 and stands on all 17s, including soft 17. Players compare with the dealer, not with one another. Splitting, surrender, and insurance are not offered.

Settlement and the next round

An ordinary win pays one-to-one profit. A two-card natural blackjack pays three-to-two profit unless the dealer also has a natural; equal ordinary totals or equal naturals return the bet. A natural beats a non-natural 21, and a busted player loses even if the dealer later busts.

Every new round uses a fresh shuffled one- or two-deck shoe. The active host starts the next round. A player unable to cover the fixed bet cannot continue; the table ends when no active player can cover it. Leaving forfeits an outstanding wager.

An ace gives you room to move

You receive ace and six, a soft 17. If you hit and draw a nine, the ace changes to one and the total becomes 16, not 26. Standing now keeps that 16 while the dealer completes its hand.

For a separate payout example, an ace and king make a natural blackjack. With the 50-chip wager, a winning natural returns the stake plus 75 profit. An ordinary 21 made with three cards does not receive that natural-blackjack bonus.

Practice the rules, not a promise of profit

  • Separate soft totals from hard ones before deciding whether another card can bust you.
  • Doubling commits one more wager and only one more card; consider both consequences.
  • Look at the dealer's visible card, but do not assume you know the hidden one.
  • A fresh shoe each round means earlier rounds do not reveal a persistent remaining deck.
