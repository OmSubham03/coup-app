Bring your word-building friends

  1. Select Play Scrabble now, create a room, and choose a display name.
  2. Share its five-character code with one to three friends, or enable public discovery for available seats.
  3. The host starts with two to four players. Select tiles from your private rack, place them on the board, and submit when the arrangement is ready.

No account, download, or dictionary API key is needed. A saved session reconnects before a new link opens another setup.

Build connected words, not isolated islands

The first move must cover the center square. On a turn, place one to seven rack tiles in a single row or column without gaps, using existing tiles to connect later moves. Every new main or crossing word must be valid; a one-letter arrangement without a longer word is not enough.

Points and blanks

Letters have individual values. Double/triple letter squares multiply a newly placed letter, and double/triple word squares multiply the new word. Premiums apply only when their square receives a new tile, not every time someone reuses that letter. Playing all seven rack tiles adds 50 points.

A blank can represent a chosen letter but remains worth zero, even on a letter multiplier. Once placed, that letter assignment stays on the board. Your rack refills toward seven from the remaining bag after an accepted move.

Dictionary checks and house voting

The bundled public-domain ENABLE lexicon checks new words; it is not an official tournament dictionary. An unknown word sends the proposed move to a house vote. The proposer counts as approving, and every other active player must approve. One rejection returns the proposal without consuming the turn.

A vote cannot override invalid placement, missing rack tiles, disconnected words, or gaps. The table judges unfamiliar words, not whether the geometric move rules apply.

Passing, exchanging, and finishing

You may pass, or exchange one to seven rack tiles when the bag has at least seven tiles; either uses your turn. The game ends when someone goes out with an empty bag, or after six consecutive scoreless turns. Leftover tile values are deducted; a player who goes out also collects the others' leftover values. Highest final score wins.

A short word can use the center well

On an empty board, place C-A-T horizontally with A on the center double-word square. C scores three, A one, and T one: five before the multiplier, or ten points for the opening word.

If A came from a blank, its value would be zero, making the same placement worth eight. A later player building a crossing word through that A does not reuse the center's double-word bonus because the center tile is already on the board.

Score this turn without ruining the next

  • Inspect every cross-word before submitting a long main word.
  • Balance consonants and vowels instead of keeping only high-value letters.
  • Short words that make several crossings can outperform a long isolated word.
  • Agree on how you will judge unknown words, and save blanks for flexible placements rather than assuming they score like printed letters.
