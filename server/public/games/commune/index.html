How high can the claim go?

A pair becomes three of a kind. Three becomes a straight. Eventually someone must decide whether the table really contains that hand. Your private cards are clues, not the whole answer.

Gather the hidden hands

  1. Choose Play Commune now, create a room, and choose a display name.
  2. Send the five-character code to friends, or enable a public room so others can find available seats.
  3. The host starts with two to ten players. Each player sees their own hand and the two shared community cards.

Tokens are lives, not money. There is no download or sign-in requirement. A saved game in the same tab reconnects before a new game link opens.

Declare higher or call the last claim

The deck contains 52 cards, with all twos acting as wild cards. Each player starts with five tokens. Your hand size is six minus your remaining tokens: one card at five tokens, two at four, and so on. Losing a life gives you more private information in the next deal.

On your turn, declare a hand stronger than the last declaration, or call the previous claim. Claims are checked across all dealt cards: the two community cards plus every living player's hand. You do not need to hold the entire claimed hand yourself.

The declaration ladder

From low to high: high card, pair, two pair, three of a kind, straight, full house, four of a kind, two trips, ultra straight, four plus three, and five of a kind. Ordinary flushes and straight flushes are not declaration categories here. Ultra straight means all ranks from three through ace.

Within a category, the selected primary rank and then secondary rank determine whether a claim is higher. Use the picker to choose the category and any required ranks; selecting the category alone does not submit the declaration.

What a call resolves

All dealt cards are revealed. If the claimed hand exists, the caller loses one token; if it does not, the last declarer loses one. At zero tokens a player is eliminated. The last living player wins.

For the next round, the deck is reshuffled, two new community cards are dealt, and hand sizes reflect remaining tokens. The previous loser begins if still alive; otherwise the next living player begins. The declaration ladder resets.

A missing card might be in someone else's hand

The shared cards are a king and a two. Your private card is another king. A claim of three kings can be true: the two acts as a third king. A rival who sees only the shared cards may underestimate that claim.

If another player raises to four kings and you call, the server checks every hidden hand as well. A king held by a third player can make the claim valid and cost you a token. Your own visible subset cannot prove that a higher claim is impossible.

Think about the whole deal

  • Count shared wilds before judging how demanding a declaration is.
  • More lost tokens means more cards dealt to that player, not a smaller hand.
  • Read the full named declaration and rank selection before raising; the category ladder differs from regular poker.
  • A call can save you from an impossible raise, but unseen cards are part of the evidence too.
