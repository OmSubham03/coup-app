  3. The host starts only when all four players have joined. A public room can help fill empty seats when the host enables discovery.

Game points are not cash. No download is required, and following a game link does not replace a saved session in the same tab.

Bid for points, not just high cards

Each suit uses J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, and 7, ranked in that order from strongest to weakest. J is worth three points, 9 two, A one, and 10 one; the other ranks score zero. The deck contains 28 card points, plus one for the last trick.

Bidding and hidden trump

You see four cards before bidding. Legal bids range from 15 to 28 and must beat the current bid. The highest bidder selects a trump suit represented in their first four cards. After that choice, four more cards are dealt to each seat.

Follow the led suit when possible. A player unable to follow can request trump reveal. Before revelation, the hidden suit does not beat the led suit merely because it was selected; after revelation, trump cards can win over non-trumps.

If you are void in the led suit and choose to trump, you must beat a trump already played when you can; you may instead discard a non-trump. This is not an unconditional rule forcing every void player to trump. If trump has still not been revealed after seven completed tricks, the hand is annulled.

Pairs and the match

After trump is revealed, a player holding its king and queen can declare the pair. A bidding-team pair lowers the target by four, to a minimum of 15; an opposing pair raises it by four, to a maximum of 28.

Meeting the target earns the bidding team one game point; failing costs one. The loss becomes two points when the team's card points are below the original bid divided by two, rounded down. A team reaching plus six wins; a team reaching minus six loses. The winner of a trick leads the next one.

A small card can decide a big bid

Your team bids 20 and selects hearts as trump. After trump is revealed, the king and queen of hearts are declared by your partnership. Your effective target falls to 16.

In a club-led trick, you must play a club if you have one. The jack of clubs beats the ace of clubs in this game. If you have no clubs, a heart can win once trump is revealed; those trick points then contribute to your partnership's target.

Let the partnership do the work

  • Learn the J-9-A-10 order before bidding; familiar poker rankings will mislead you.
  • Estimate useful trump control as well as the point cards in your first four cards.
  • Watch which suits opponents can no longer follow, and remember that your partner shares the target.
  • Do not delay trump reveal indefinitely: an unrevealed hand is annulled after the seventh trick.
